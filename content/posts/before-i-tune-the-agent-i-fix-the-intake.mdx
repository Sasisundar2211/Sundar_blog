My AI agent failed not because the model was weak, but because the data entering the workflow was broken.

In my third year of B.Tech AIML, I started building FirmRunner after watching accounting firms collect client records across WhatsApp messages, email threads, and printed forms that staff still retyped into spreadsheets by hand.

Everyone around me was focused on training smarter models. In practice, companies do not lose money because a model scored 73% instead of 91%. They lose money when the intake breaks at step two and sends the wrong output to the wrong person.

That is not an intelligence problem. It is a systems problem.

A model with 98% accuracy still fails completely when the input it receives is malformed or incomplete. Automating the output while ignoring broken input only accelerates errors.

Before I tune a prompt or swap a model, I ask one question: where does the information first become unreliable?

Fix the intake first, validate the schema at entry, and the downstream reasoning becomes dramatically simpler.
